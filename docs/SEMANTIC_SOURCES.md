## November 2025: primary letter inspected

The two-page [12 November 2025 letter scan](https://assets.documentcloud.org/documents/26229389/adobe-scan-nov-12-2025.pdf) is linked from [Scientific American's eyewitness report](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/cia-kryptos-puzzle-creator-releases-final-clues/). Both rendered pages were visually checked because the PDF's text extraction contains OCR errors.

Page 1 identifies K4's clock as the **“World Clock in Berlin”**, associates it with the crowds and the Wall, and relates the work to Sanborn's late-1986 Egypt visit and the Wall's fall. It characterizes the Morse-to-K5 sequence as **“delivering a message”**, then connects the continuation to K2's buried-message wording. His discussion of leaving something at the CIA site is conditional, not a declaration of a recoverable buried object. The letter's stated 1988 writing date and reference to the Wall's later fall are preserved as a chronology inconsistency; this note does not repair them.

Page 2 describes K5 as a surviving alternative ending: 97 characters, a coding system similar to K4, and the words BERLINCLOCK in the same position. The sentence does not explicitly say *plaintext position*; relating it to the known K4 plaintext crib is a contextual inference. It proposes future public placement because K5 is not on the CIA screen. This supplies a stated shared feature, not the K5 ciphertext or its complete semantics. The proposed 78-character contraction cannot by itself be an identification of this announced 97-character K5; a further relationship would have to be specified.

## What this distinguishes

1. **Literal referent:** World Clock has explicit creator support. Turning BERLINCLOCK into BC remains an unconfirmed extraction rule; it cannot discard that referent without an explanation.
2. **Direction:** EASTNORTHEAST consists of confirmed plaintext letters. Reading the phrase as a compass direction is plausible; this bounded review found no creator instruction fixing its starting point, distance, geographic frame, or conversion to ENE. A direction, abbreviation, and text-reading route are separate hypotheses.
3. **Additional meaning:** The creator's riddle statements and Scheidt's example support investigating a further meaning. They do not select initials, abbreviations, a phonetic alphabet, or an actual codebook.
4. **Discriminating evidence:** Prefer a rule that predicts a new, independently checkable instruction or symbol using fixed word boundaries. Selecting flexible abbreviations after seeing a desired output would provide little evidence.

The confirmed K4 crib positions stay unchanged. No language scoring, cipher search, external submission, or message to the artist or owner was performed for this review. No speculative forum solution was used as evidence. Absence of an abbreviation rule in these selected sources is not a proof that no such rule exists.
