# Proposed second layer of meaning

User hypothesis, recorded after checkpoint K4-20261012-E: K4's literal decoded words might be converted to abbreviations or symbols, for example EASTNORTHEAST to ENE and BERLINCLOCK to BC, with a further hidden message or instructions emerging afterward. This is an open hypothesis, not a decipherment.

The proposed stages are:

```text
97-letter sculpture ciphertext
    -> cryptographic decryption
97-letter literal plaintext containing the confirmed words
    -> justified abbreviation / codebook / instruction interpretation
further message or instructions, potentially relevant to K5
```

The released clues constrain the literal plaintext: EASTNORTHEAST occupies positions 22–34 and BERLINCLOCK occupies 64–74, counting from one. A downstream interpretation must not silently replace these with shorter crib strings in the existing cryptographic tests. Earlier exact exclusions remain conditional on the original confirmed letters and apply unchanged to this staged hypothesis.

ENE is the conventional abbreviation for the compass direction east-northeast. BC is a proposed initialism of Berlin Clock, not an authenticated symbol or key. Treating a compass direction as a bearing, taking initials, or using a phonetic alphabet are different extraction rules until a common rule is specified. For example, a standard phonetic spelling system uses designated words for letters; arbitrary words beginning with those letters are not automatically its codewords.

If only these two substrings in a 97-letter plaintext are replaced and everything else is preserved, the intermediate length is 97−13−11+3+2 = 78 letters. This arithmetic is a consequence of the proposal, not evidence that a 78-letter hidden passage exists. The unknown surrounding 73 letters prevent computing that proposed intermediate message now.

There is relevant primary-source context: in his [2005 Wired interview](https://www.wired.com/2005/01/inside-info-on-kryptos-codes/), Ed Scheidt describes a readable message carrying an additional coded meaning, illustrated through an agreed everyday word for a meeting time. That supports considering a semantic layer in principle; it does not authenticate ENE, BC, an acrostic, or a specific relationship to the later K5 material.

## Discriminating checks

1. Specify one extraction rule before interpreting a candidate: conventional abbreviations, initial letters under fixed word boundaries, or an independently supplied codebook. Preserve alternatives as separate hypotheses.
2. Require the same rule to work across the entire recovered message; avoid changing abbreviations, segmentation, language, or reading order to manufacture an expected phrase.
3. Check whether the output supplies independently checkable instructions or a constrained second cipher. A short suggestive fragment by itself has many accidental explanations.
4. Use evidence from the rest of the sculpture to justify any codebook, ordering, or word boundaries. Successful decryption and semantic interpretation are separate obligations.
